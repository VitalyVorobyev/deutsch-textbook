# `textbook-quality-rubric.md`

## Purpose

This rubric evaluates explanatory text written in the learner's native language for a foreign-language textbook.

It does not evaluate whether the foreign-language facts are correct. Its focus is whether the explanation is:

- understandable on the first reading;
- natural in the target editorial language;
- unambiguous;
- instructionally useful;
- economical without becoming incomplete;
- appropriate for the learner's assumed prior knowledge.

## Quality dimensions

Score every dimension from 0 to 4.

### 1. Clarity

- **4:** Sentences are immediately understandable.
- **3:** Minor friction, but no rereading is normally required.
- **2:** Several sentences require rereading.
- **1:** The explanation is consistently difficult to parse.
- **0:** The intended meaning cannot be reconstructed reliably.

Check for:

- excessive sentence length;
- nested clauses;
- nominalizations;
- vague abstractions;
- unnecessary passive constructions;
- several claims compressed into one sentence.

### 2. Referential precision

- **4:** Every pronoun and reference has one obvious interpretation.
- **3:** One minor reference could be clearer.
- **2:** Several references require inference.
- **1:** Ambiguity can produce incorrect understanding.
- **0:** Core statements have no stable interpretation.

Flag words such as:

- this;
- that;
- it;
- such;
- the former;
- the latter;
- in this case;
- similarly.

Do not flag them when their referent is explicit.

The flag-word list is language-generic — transpose it to the editorial language under review (for Russian: «это», «тот», «такой», «в этом случае», «последний»; likewise for Ukrainian).

### 3. Coherence

- **4:** Each statement follows naturally from the previous one.
- **3:** One transition is implicit but recoverable.
- **2:** The reader must supply several missing connections.
- **1:** Ideas are presented in a confusing order.
- **0:** The text contradicts itself or lacks a discernible structure.

Prefer this instructional sequence when applicable:

1. learner-oriented meaning;
2. central usage condition;
3. contrast with a likely alternative;
4. example;
5. limitation or exception;
6. short retrieval or application task.

### 4. Cognitive economy

- **4:** Every part supports the immediate learning objective.
- **3:** A small amount of nonessential material remains.
- **2:** The main point competes with secondary details.
- **1:** Redundancy or premature exceptions obscure the main rule.
- **0:** The learner cannot identify what must be remembered.

Flag:

- repeated explanations;
- decorative introductions;
- premature terminology;
- multiple exceptions before the basic rule;
- examples that introduce unrelated difficulties.

### 5. Instructional sufficiency

- **4:** The learner can recognize and apply the distinction.
- **3:** The learner can understand it but may need one more example.
- **2:** The text states information without enabling application.
- **1:** The educational objective is only implied.
- **0:** No identifiable learning outcome is supported.

A useful explanation normally answers:

- What does this mean here?
- When is it used?
- What nearby alternative could be confused with it?
- How can the learner choose between them?
- What does a typical example look like?

Not every passage must answer all five questions. Judge them against its stated purpose.

### 6. Naturalness

- **4:** Reads like professionally edited native-language educational prose.
- **3:** Correct but slightly mechanical in one or two places.
- **2:** Noticeably translated, bureaucratic, or model-generated.
- **1:** Frequently unnatural.
- **0:** Not acceptable native-language prose.

Flag:

- calques from the language being taught;
- bureaucratic wording;
- unnatural collocations;
- unnecessary explicitness;
- repetitive sentence patterns;
- artificial transitions.

### 7. Meaning preservation

Use this dimension only when comparing an original and a revision.

- **4:** All instructional claims and distinctions are preserved.
- **3:** Minor nonessential nuance was removed.
- **2:** One relevant qualification was weakened or altered.
- **1:** A central claim changed.
- **0:** The revision teaches something materially different.

## Acceptance rules

A text passes only when:

- no dimension is below 3;
- clarity, referential precision, and meaning preservation are at least 4 for definitions and rules;
- no unresolved critical issue remains;
- the central learning objective can be stated in one sentence;
- at least one comprehension or application question can be answered from the text.

Do not average away a serious defect.

## Quantitative diagnostics

Use these as warning signals, not as automatic quality judgments:

- median sentence length above 18 words;
- more than 10% of sentences above 25 words;
- more than one major proposition per sentence;
- more than three new concepts before an example;
- an example separated from its rule by more than one paragraph;
- repeated explanation of the same point;
- undefined instructional terminology;
- long chains of nouns;
- two or more plausible antecedents for a pronoun.

A text may exceed a threshold when the wording remains demonstrably clear.
