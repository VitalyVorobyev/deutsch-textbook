# A2–B1 curriculum contract

What A2 and B1 teach, which unit owns what, and what each unit may not touch. The rules for writing
any single artifact are in [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md); the bar a unit clears is the unit gate in
[the content-quality contract](../authoring/content-quality-audit.md#the-unit-gate). The topic
manifests and `content/atlas.yaml` are the record of what shipped; this file is the record of why
each unit owns what it owns.

**Identities are frozen.** A unit id, topic id, outcome id, focus tag, deck id, set path-id or
reading id becomes a persisted key in the learner's progress the moment it ships, and renaming it
destroys their history. Insert, never renumber. Section numbers such as B1.4 are document labels,
not identities.

## Progression principles

- A2 moves the learner from rehearsed A1 exchanges to independent handling of predictable everyday
  situations; B1 grows discourse length, independence and genre range — it is not simply more
  grammar.
- Grammar is embedded in the situation that needs it, and gets its own node only when the contrast
  is reusable across many situations.
- Every unit centres on one scenario (the mission rule of the unit gate), 2–4 observable *Ich kann …*
  outcomes, and the lesson cycle pretest → model → explanation → scaffold → fade → transfer →
  delayed check.
- Earlier language is revisited through `deepens` edges and cumulative retrieval. No unit assumes a
  structure met once was retained.
- An outcome's `mode` is what the curriculum targets, not what the learner did: attempts record
  their own `responseMode`.

## Vocabulary

**A2: recycle, never adopt.** An A2 unit never lists an existing A1 deck in its `vocab:` — that
flips the deck's fresh-card gate from "any A1 topic opened" to "this topic opened" and buries A1
words behind A2. It recycles the A1 deck in its article, reading and exercises, and owns one thin
`level: A2` deck (12–24 entries) of language no deck already teaches, chunks included. Receptive-only
language (announcements, listing abbreviations, form headings) goes in readings, glosses and tables,
never in an A2 deck: A2 decks are two-card and are never retrofitted.

**B1: two tiers, decided once.** A unit deck holds 30–40 entries. The core the unit teaches for
production ships `cards: both`; everything beyond it ships `cards: recognition` from the start.
Never flip a shipped entry: the direction is in the card id.

**Completion decks are unowned.** Each level's Wortliste tail lives in decks of `level: <level>`
that no topic lists in `vocab:`, gated by level rather than by a topic. Run
`bun scripts/coverage.ts <level> --check-deck <file>` per deck before `bun run validate`; it rejects
any entry another deck already owns. Count entries by parsing the YAML, never by grepping a key at a
fixed indentation.

**A word gets a card if and only if a learner could plausibly produce it.** Rarity is not a licence
to mark `~`:

- **No card, earned as `~` by a German table or reading:** bound stems (`eigen-`, `beid-`),
  compound first elements (`Bio-`, `Haupt-`), affixes (`un-`, `-weise`), two-part frames
  (`je … desto …`), written abbreviations (`z. B.`, `bzw.`, `EG`), the number-formation demos, and
  spelling variants whose card would interfere with the main form (`Disko`/`Disco`).
- **Cards, against the temptation:** spoken abbreviations with gender and plural (`WC`, `Lkw`),
  general words that merely also appear in rubrics (`Punkt`, `Text`), all professions, countries and
  school subjects, feminine agent nouns (recognition, beside their masculine), and Austrian and Swiss
  variants (recognition: different words, not spellings).
- **The matcher folds every non-letter to a space**, so `Bioladen` is one token and earns nothing,
  while `Bio-Laden` or the bare cell `Bio-` earns `Bio-`. A table paying off compound elements
  hyphenates.
- **Passing the earned-`~` check is not being taught.** `lieb-` once passed off the comparative
  *lieber*, `Not-` off *Noten*, `rück-` off *Rücken*. Each stem needs a cell that teaches it.

## Probe families

- One three-variant family per competence a unit owns, cloze preferred, each with its own explicit
  `arming:` list. A new family cannot move an existing family's clock, but capture `armedAt` before
  and after anyway.
- **A family arms by outcome.** Two families that declare the same outcome arm identically, so a
  unit owing N families needs N distinct outcomes among them, and outcomes are capped at four per
  node. Arming is not focus-aware: within one outcome, an unrelated item can start a family's clock
  early.
- **An outcome added to separate arming is still an outcome** and owes a task in the mode it names.

## A2

Shipped, and its topic pass is complete ([ledger](topic-quality-audit.md)). A2 ends with a cumulative checkpoint that samples the level's
outcomes; finishing it is not a claim that the learner speaks A2 German. B1 readiness means
connecting several sentences, recovering from a predictable misunderstanding, and still having the
high-value A2 language weeks later.

## B1

**Grammar ownership is exhaustive.** Each of the 32 B1 points in `data/grammar-inventory.yaml` is
owned by exactly one unit below (3+3+4+3+3+3+2+2+3+3+0+0+0+3 = 32), carried by 35 focus tags.
Only a new B1 tag closes a B1 point; recycled A2 tags carry the `deepens` edges and never count.
The genre units B1.11–B1.13 own no point and leave the `tests/grammar-coverage.test.ts` ratchet
unchanged. B1 may revisit an A2 structure at greater depth — the dative relative pronoun, the
produced passive, *obwohl* as a conjunction — but never re-teaches it. Command:
`bun scripts/grammar-coverage.ts B1`.

Every B1 topic carries `<En>`, `<Ru>`, `<Uk>` and `<De>` halves. Pronunciation shifts toward
clause grouping, sentence accent, reductions and connected speech.

The fourteen contracted units, in path order. The manifest holds each unit's outcomes and
`deepens` edges.

### B1.1 · `erfahrungen-erzaehlen` — Erfahrungen erzählen

**Mission:** tell a connected story, understand an interview, write a narrative message.
**Grammar:** `praeteritum-vollverben` → `praeteritum-vollverben`; `plusquamperfekt` →
`plusquamperfekt-nachdem`; `temporalsatz` → `temporal-nebensatz`. The Perfekt–Präteritum register
split is the teaching point.
**Exclude:** the Plusquamperfekt as a free narrative tense — it is practised after *nachdem* and in
*schon … als* — literary narration, indirect speech (B1.14).

### B1.2 · `leben-veraendern` — Leben verändern

**Mission:** discuss housing and relocation, compare options, justify a choice.
**Grammar:** `relativsatz-dativ` → `relativpronomen-dativ`, `relativ-praeposition`;
`komparativ-attributiv` → `komparativ-attributiv`; `genitiv-vollstaendig` → `genitiv-form`.
**Exclude:** tenancy law, dessen/deren in production, attributive superlatives beyond fixed phrases.

### B1.3 · `gesundheit-wohlbefinden` — Gesundheit & Wohlbefinden

**Mission:** explain a history, understand recommendations, discuss habits; never diagnostic.
**Grammar:** `reflexiv-praeposition` → `reflexiv-praeposition`; `konjunktiv2-ratschlag` →
`konjunktiv2-ratschlag` (the productive paradigm behind the A2 chunks — `hoeflich-konjunktiv` never
closes it); `lassen` → `lassen-verwendung`; `adjektiv-nullartikel` → `adjektiv-nullartikel`.
**Exclude:** diagnoses, medical terminology beyond everyday complaints, the unreal Konjunktiv II
paradigm (B1.8).

### B1.4 · `arbeit-bewerbung` — Arbeit & Bewerbung

**Mission:** read a vacancy, present experience, write an application, handle interview turns.
**Grammar:** `n-deklination` → `n-deklination`; `adjektiv-nomen` → `adjektiv-nomen`;
`nomen-verb-verbindungen` → `nomen-verb-verbindung`.
**Exclude:** contract and labour-law language, CV-format conventions as content, salary negotiation.

### B1.5 · `meinung-medien` — Meinung & Medien

**Mission:** understand a report, summarize its point, support an opinion.
**Grammar:** `verb-praeposition-erweitert` → `verb-praeposition-b1`; `kausalsatz-da` → `da-weil`;
`zweiteilige-konnektoren` → `zweiteilige-konnektoren` (*entweder … oder*, *nicht nur … sondern
auch*, *zwar … aber* — *je … desto* is B1.6's).
**Exclude:** politics and news beyond everyday media use, opinion essays.

### B1.6 · `konsum-umwelt` — Konsum & Umwelt

**Mission:** compare choices and understand how things are made, sold and regulated.
**Grammar:** `passiv-produktion` → `passiv-bildung`; `passiv-vergangenheit` →
`passiv-vergangenheit`; `proportionalsatz-je-desto` → `je-desto` (a subordinate clause with its own
word order, not a two-part connector).
**Exclude:** Vorgangs- vs Zustandspassiv terminology, policy debate, statistics, the modal passive
(B1.7).

### B1.7 · `regeln-verantwortung` — Regeln & Verantwortung

**Mission:** understand what must, may and cannot be done, state what follows, negotiate a
practical solution.
**Grammar:** `passiv-modal` → `passiv-modal`; `konsekutivsatz-sodass` → `sodass-folge`.
**Exclude:** legal obligation language, the split *so … dass* variant, Zustandspassiv.

### B1.8 · `reisen-probleme` — Reisen & Probleme

**Mission:** manage less predictable disruption and make a complaint.
**Grammar:** `konjunktiv2-irreal` → `konjunktiv2-form`, `irreale-bedingung`;
`praeposition-genitiv` → `praeposition-genitiv`.
**Exclude:** Konjunktiv II of full verbs beyond hätte/wäre/würde and the core modals, compensation
law.

### B1.9 · `lernen-zukunft` — Lernen & Zukunft

**Mission:** discuss learning and career goals, summarize, plan next steps.
**Grammar:** `finalsatz-damit` → `damit-um-zu`; `konditionalsatz-falls` → `falls-wenn`;
`infinitivsatz-ohne-statt` → `ohne-statt-zu`.
**Exclude:** formal study-counselling vocabulary, Futur II, career-planning jargon.

### B1.10 · `gesellschaft-zusammenleben` — Gesellschaft & Zusammenleben

**Mission:** join a familiar community discussion and resolve a disagreement; never a civics
lecture.
**Grammar:** `konzessivsatz-obwohl` → `obwohl-trotzdem`; `indefinitpronomen-erweitert` →
`indefinitpronomen-erweitert`; `relativ-was-wo` → `relativ-was-wo`.
**Exclude:** political institutions, migration-policy content, formal debate structure.

### B1.11 · `digitales-leben` — Digitales Leben

**Genre unit, no grammar point.** Recycles `passiv-bildung` and `sodass-folge`.
**Mission:** follow written instructions, get help when something went wrong, talk about media
habits. Genres: Anleitung, Hilfetext, Online-Formular.
**Exclude:** IT-specialist vocabulary, data-protection law, brand language.

### B1.12 · `kultur-freizeit` — Kultur & Freizeit

**Genre unit, no grammar point.** Recycles `komparativ-attributiv` and `praeteritum-vollverben`.
**Mission:** read what is on, agree on a plan, say what an event was like. Genres: Programm,
Rezension, Einladung.
**Exclude:** art-criticism register, sports-reporting jargon, cultural history.

### B1.13 · `geld-vertraege` — Geld & Verträge

**Genre unit, no grammar point.** Recycles `genitiv-form`, `passiv-modal` and `n-deklination`.
**Mission:** check a bill, clarify a contract, compare costs. Genres: Rechnung, Vertrag,
Kostenvoranschlag.
**Exclude:** banking and insurance law, investment vocabulary, tax content.

### B1.14 · `informationen-vermitteln` — Informationen vermitteln

**Mission:** relay the main points of a notice, message or conversation — the mediation unit that
closes the level.
**Grammar:** `indirekte-rede` → `indirekte-rede` (Konjunktiv I receptive only);
`wortstellung-angaben` → `angaben-reihenfolge`, `pronomen-stellung`; `partizip-adjektiv` →
`partizip-adjektiv`.
**Exclude:** Konjunktiv I production, journalistic register, minutes and protocol formats.

### Beyond the contract

Six further B1 topics close the DTZ Themen and Sprachhandlungen denominators and own no grammar
point: `unfall-notruf`, `polizei-anzeige`, `versicherungen-behoerden`, `sucht-lebensstil`,
`bildungswege`, `gespraech-steuern`. Their scope is the claims their manifests make
(`bun scripts/themen.ts B1`, `bun scripts/handlungen.ts B1`).
