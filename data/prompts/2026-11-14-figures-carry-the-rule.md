# Figures carry the rule — redraw of five weak views

Owner's brief, 2026-11-14. Vitaly Vorobyev said the figures in topic articles "are not really good
and helpful", with *seit oder vor?* as the example, and set the bar: a figure exists to clarify a
concept that can be expressed visually, never because a figure was planned. He approved the redrawn
*seit oder vor?* from screenshots and left the verdict on every other figure to Claude Code.

The test applied to all 18 uses: **take the words away — does the drawing still say the rule?**

Redrawn, as deterministic HTML, CSS and inline SVG, no raster artwork:

- `TimeRelationFigure`, view `since-before`: one axis from Montag to jetzt for both rows. *seit* is
  a solid bar that reaches now, *vor* a single point and an empty dashed stretch. Each row carries
  the article's own sentence, the verb marked and the tense named.
- `TimeRelationFigure`, view `clock`: all twelve numbers; the short hand halfway between 8 and 9,
  the 9 in the hand's colour. The um / am / im cards are gone — they repeated the table directly
  above the figure and were no drawing. The digits no longer overlap the hands.
- `SentenceRail`, all seven views: the single boxed sentence becomes two or three sentences in
  shared columns. What the rule fixes stays in its column from row to row (the verb on Position 2,
  the two halves of the bracket); what the rule moves leaves a dashed empty slot behind (the verb
  of a weil clause and of an indirect question).
- `ParticipantRoleFigure`, view `pain`: the arrow ran from the person to the body part, the
  opposite of the grammar. It now runs from the body part (subject) through *tut / tun weh* to the
  person (Dativ).

Kept unchanged: `ParticipantRoleFigure` views `giving` and `body-care`, `PronominalAdverbFlow`,
`NarrativeTimelineFigure`, both `RouteMovementFigure` views and both `WohnenWoWohin` views.

Every redrawn view keeps a German caption and a complete EN/RU/UK text equivalent, reads without
colour, and fits a 320 px screen in both themes.
